# Security Review — Round 1 Named-Agent Runtime Artifacts

## Verdict

**No production deployment. Conditional pilot only, after the controls and gate evidence below exist.** The accepted Round 1 design is directionally sound—Hermes inside Maya's existing Orgo computer as the sole baseline owner, followed by an isolated eve comparison—but the evidence is largely design/configuration, not verified enforcement. The current combined risk is **P0** because a Slack- or mailbox-delivered prompt injection could reach an authenticated browser or over-scoped credential, cross an ambiguous approval boundary, and produce an externally attributable action while the audit trail and recovery owner remain uncertain.

Hermes/Orgo and eve have **not passed** the repository's third-party-agent-tool gate. Cursor and Devin are unsuitable as Roofing-Ops persona runtimes; at most they may receive separate, later gate reviews for a low-risk DevTeam or browser-adjunct pilot. No artifact establishes current production listener, scheduler, OAuth, inbox, tenant-isolation, or end-to-end receipt health (`../../round1/linear-runtime-forensics/runtime-forensics.md`, “Executive Verdict,” “Evidence,” and “Unknowns”).

## Threat Model

Protected assets are customer and financial data, Google mail and browser sessions, persona identity, Slack work intake, approvals, Command Center records, provider prompts, secrets, logs/receipts, and the Historian/Researcher boundary. Trust boundaries are Slack users/apps/workspaces; Gmail and AgentMail inboxes; Orgo workspaces/computers; Hermes/eve sandboxes and skills; model providers; Command Center/Supabase; Cursor/Devin vendor planes; and human approval surfaces.

Threat actors include a malicious or compromised Slack member, spoofed or compromised bot/app, external sender, malicious attachment/webpage, compromised OAuth grant or service account, hostile dependency/skill/update, vendor or cross-tenant failure, compromised worker/browser profile, and an operator mistakenly enabling two trigger owners. The principal attack chains are:

1. Untrusted Slack, email, attachment, or webpage content supplies instructions that the model treats as authority; the runtime uses mailbox, browser, shell, or internal-data tools to exfiltrate or act.
2. A missing persona token falls back to a shared Slack bot, or a shared service inbox loses actor attribution, enabling impersonation and repudiation.
3. A broad OAuth/service credential or persistent browser session converts one compromised worker into cross-mailbox, cross-persona, or cross-tenant access.
4. Approval is omitted, auto-continued, bypassed through an API flag, replayed after edits, or enforced only in prompt text rather than at the side-effect boundary.
5. Two listeners/schedulers concurrently own the same event, causing duplicate sends, writes, or conflicting receipts.
6. Secrets or sensitive bodies leak through prompts, screenshots, recordings, shell history, environment dumps, traces, durable checkpoints, vendor telemetry, or long-lived logs.
7. Recovery starts a replacement worker before fencing/revoking the compromised one, preserving attacker access or creating two active owners.

The review assumes all inbound content is adversarial, all model output is untrusted, display names are non-authoritative, browser state is a credential, and a vendor's approval UI or sandbox claim is not an Open Brain authorization boundary. These assumptions follow `../../round1/cursor-devin-fit/cursor-devin-fit.md` (“Security and Identity”), `../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md` (“Email Design,” “Slack Design,” and “Security Controls”), and `../../../../../../CONVENTIONS.md` §4.

## Findings

### P0

**P0-1 — Untrusted ingress can reach high-consequence capabilities without demonstrated policy enforcement.** Slack, email, attachments, and browser pages are prompt-injection sources. Cursor explicitly warns of exfiltration through internet-enabled auto-running agents; Devin preserves browser authentication state; eve HITL is permissive when omitted; and Hermes controls are proposed but not proven. No accepted artifact supplies adversarial injection results showing that content cannot invoke shell, browser, internal retrieval, external send, permission change, or secret-bearing tools. A prompt prohibition is insufficient (`../../round1/cursor-devin-fit/cursor-devin-fit.md:18-20,29-32,57`; `../../round1/eve-fit/eve-fit.md`, “Verified Capabilities”; `../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Security Controls”).

**P0-2 — Principal isolation and impersonation prevention are contradictory.** The runtime forensics found a silent fallback from persona Slack identity to shared `@openbrain`; four named personas share the Accounting service inbox; Sam routes through Conductor; and the repo contains conflicting one-app/read-only and eight-app/two-way Slack designs. A missing token can therefore produce a valid-looking reply from the wrong principal, while shared mailbox attribution can be forged or lost. Slack display names and bot renaming are not authorization boundaries (`../../round1/linear-runtime-forensics/runtime-forensics.md`, “Contradictions” 1, 2, and 10; `../../round1/cursor-devin-fit/cursor-devin-fit.md`, “Security and Identity”).

**P0-3 — Credential blast radius is not acceptably bounded.** Round 1 infers that the observed Orgo credential is account-wide, not Maya-workspace-scoped. Current Google OAuth scopes, service-account/domain-wide delegation, Slack scopes, AgentMail webhook scope, Command Center bearer permissions, and browser-profile isolation are unverified. Compromise of Maya's persistent worker could therefore become cross-workspace or cross-system compromise. The Maya artifact correctly requires rotation, positive/negative scope tests, per-persona credentials, and no Supabase service-role token, but those are target controls rather than evidence (`../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Orgo Integration,” “Named Agent Template,” and “Security Controls”; `../../round1/linear-runtime-forensics/runtime-forensics.md`, “Unknowns”).

### P1

**P1-1 — Approval can be omitted, bypassed, replayed, or confused with identity.** Eve requires explicit HITL configuration; Devin exposes `bypass_approval` and can auto-continue planning; Cursor cloud commands auto-run. No live proof shows that approval is enforced server-side for every consequential effect, that the approver is authorized and distinct from the requester/agent, or that approval binds immutable sender, recipients, payload/attachment hashes, expiry, and idempotency key. The repository's zero-external-send rule therefore remains unproven at the tool/API boundary (`../../round1/eve-fit/eve-fit.md`, “Verified Capabilities”; `../../round1/cursor-devin-fit/cursor-devin-fit.md:20,32,46`; `../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Email Design”; `../../../../../../context/SOUL.md`, “Core Truths”).

**P1-2 — Mailbox scope and sender authenticity are unresolved.** Native Gmail parity is unverified for eve, Cursor, and Devin. The proposed Gmail design appropriately uses per-persona OAuth, label/query filters, and API-first access, but no scope manifest, unrelated-message negative test, domain-wide-delegation exclusion, or draft-only enforcement receipt exists. Shared AgentMail department inboxes further separate mailbox identity from named persona identity (`../../round1/eve-fit/eve-fit.md`, “Verified Capabilities”; `../../round1/cursor-devin-fit/cursor-devin-fit.md:11,28`; `../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Email Design”; `../../round1/linear-runtime-forensics/runtime-forensics.md`, “Current Architecture”).

**P1-3 — Data egress and vendor tenancy have not been proven.** Orgo, Vercel/eve, model providers, Cursor, Devin, Slack, Google, and AgentMail may receive prompts, source content, metadata, recordings, traces, or credentials. Proposed provider flags and allowlists are useful but do not prove provider-side retention/training settings, subprocessor/region terms, tenant isolation, deletion, breach notification, or support-access controls. Cursor says its backend still participates even with customer-run workers; Devin's Brain remains in Cognition Cloud; eve's polished path uses multiple Vercel services (`../../round1/cursor-devin-fit/cursor-devin-fit.md:22,34,49`; `../../round1/eve-fit/eve-fit.md`, “Verified Capabilities”; `../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Model and Cost Policy”).

**P1-4 — Exclusive ownership and exactly-once effects are designed, not established.** Historical VPS/Kasm, Orgo/Hermes, and an eve pilot can overlap. Multiple schedule authorities and listeners exist, while ordinary Slack work does not reliably enter the canonical queue. Without a fenced lease and atomic effect ledger, retries or recovery can duplicate Slack replies, drafts, or writes (`../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Runtime Ownership” and “Slack Design”; `../../round1/linear-runtime-forensics/runtime-forensics.md`, “Current Architecture,” “Contradictions” 3, 5, 7, and “Failure Modes”).

**P1-5 — A compromised browser/worker lacks verified containment.** Persistent Orgo/Devin browser sessions contain cookies, local storage, downloads, and potentially MFA-established authority. No artifact proves per-persona OS ownership, profile encryption, extension policy, download quarantine, clipboard isolation, session revocation, clean restore, or that browser automation cannot click Send. Human desktop takeover can also blur actor attribution (`../../round1/cursor-devin-fit/cursor-devin-fit.md:17,29,43,57`; `../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Email Design” and “Failure and Recovery”).

### P2

**P2-1 — Secrets and sensitive logs lack a verified lifecycle.** The accepted design forbids raw secrets in logs and proposes references, fingerprints, and redaction, but there is no inventory covering creation, scope, storage, access, rotation, expiry, revocation, backup, incident rotation, or leak scanning. Eve durable checkpoints, Cursor/Devin recordings, Orgo screenshots, Hermes sessions, OTEL traces, and provider logs can retain sensitive content. Native eve retention is too short for business receipts yet may still be too broad for message bodies (`../../round1/eve-fit/eve-fit.md`, “Verified Capabilities”; `../../round1/cursor-devin-fit/cursor-devin-fit.md:18,30,44`; `../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Security Controls”).

**P2-2 — The Historian/Researcher security boundary can fail through credentials or cadence.** Rowan is external-only, but runtime forensics found an enabled task that cross-references price agreements and recurring schedules whose approval status is ambiguous. Tool absence, network policy, and separate deployment—not persona instructions—must enforce the boundary (`../../round1/linear-runtime-forensics/runtime-forensics.md`, “Contradictions” 6 and “Failure Modes”; `../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Named Agent Template”; `../../../../../../CONVENTIONS.md` §4).

**P2-3 — Monitoring can report health without security truth.** Current status is mock or historical; `/healthz` does not prove persona runtime, OAuth cursor, lease, receipt sink, or scope health. Logs that say a notification was “requested” without a send call can create false assurance. Security controls cannot be operated without current per-principal probes and immutable effect receipts (`../../round1/linear-runtime-forensics/runtime-forensics.md`, “Current Architecture,” “Failure Modes,” and “Evidence”).

### P3

**P3-1 — Identity, cadence, and inbox inventories drift.** Seven named personas, eight two-way Slack listeners, thirteen workforce roles, ten service inboxes, and multiple schedule authorities do not share one canonical registry. Drift increases misrouting and weakens incident scoping (`../../round1/linear-runtime-forensics/runtime-forensics.md`, “Persona Matrix” and “Contradictions” 5, 9, and 10).

**P3-2 — Evidence terminology risks promoting design to assurance.** “Designed,” “configured,” and “observed-live” are explicitly different, but several recommendations depend on unobserved controls. Every pilot decision must preserve those labels and date its evidence (`../../round1/linear-runtime-forensics/runtime-forensics.md`, “Executive Verdict”).

## Required Controls

Before any pilot, implement and test these controls:

1. **Ingress and prompt-injection firewall:** normalize Slack/email/web inputs as untrusted data; strip/scan attachments; do not place raw content in system/developer instructions; separate retrieval from action; label provenance; deny tool escalation from content; and test indirect injection, encoded instructions, malicious documents, poisoned webpages, fake approval text, and data-exfiltration requests.
2. **Server-side authorization:** a deterministic policy service checks authenticated tenant/team, human principal, persona, channel/mailbox, task class, data classification, requested effect, approval, and lease epoch. The model never grants authority. Deny by default.
3. **Persona identity:** unique Slack app/token, Google/OAuth principal, Orgo workspace/key/computer, Hermes home, Command Center bearer, provider budget, and browser profile per persona. Validate stable IDs, not names. Remove shared Slack fallback. A missing or mismatched identity fails closed.
4. **Mailbox least privilege:** use the narrowest Gmail scopes and label/query filters; prohibit domain-wide delegation unless separately justified and approved; separate read, label, draft, and send scopes; make send unavailable to runtimes; bind service-inbox ingestion to immutable envelope metadata and a canonical persona/work-item correlation ID.
5. **Approval integrity:** require an authorized human different from the agent and, for high-risk actions, different from the requester. Approval binds tenant, actor, persona, exact effect, destination, content/attachment hashes, expiry, and idempotency key. Edits invalidate it. Disable/deny every bypass or auto-continue flag. Approval UI callbacks are signed, replay-protected, and checked server-side.
6. **Egress control:** role-specific DNS/HTTPS allowlists; no arbitrary upload; no internal endpoint access from Rowan; provider allowlists with verified retention/training settings; redact/minimize prompts; block secrets and sensitive identifiers at egress; log destination and data class without logging content.
7. **Browser isolation:** API-first; task-specific account and destination; no admin identity; dedicated profile; no cross-profile filesystem access; extension/download policy; browser-side prompt-injection tests; short task lease; reason-coded fallback; reauthentication by a human; consequential clicks disabled or separately approved.
8. **Exactly-once ownership:** one canonical trigger/schedule registry; fenced per-agent/per-trigger lease; old owners quiesced and credentials revoked before transfer; Slack envelope and database uniqueness; atomic outbound effect reservation and receipt; retry returns the prior effect ID.
9. **Secrets:** approved secret manager/supervisor injection only; no values in repo, prompts, shell history, screenshots, recordings, checkpoints, exceptions, or logs; per-persona scope; short lifetime where possible; rotation and emergency revocation runbook; fingerprint/version only in receipts; automated secret scanning.
10. **Audit/logging:** immutable, access-controlled receipts containing tenant, principal, persona, source/event ID, policy result, approval ID, lease epoch, tool/effect, external receipt ID, config/version hash, and redaction status. Separate security logs from model context. Define retention/deletion by data class and export vendor audit events promptly.
11. **Vendor tenancy:** document organization/workspace ownership, region, encryption, subprocessors, support access, retention/deletion, training opt-out, incident notice, tenant-isolation tests, offboarding/export, and contractual data handling for each vendor.
12. **Adversarial acceptance:** zero critical errors across repeated synthetic tests for cross-tenant access, wrong-speaker Slack posts, unrelated-mail fetch, approval bypass/replay, duplicate effects, Rowan internal access, secret/log leakage, worker compromise, provider failure, and recovery fencing. Any P0/P1 test failure stops the pilot.

## Pilot Boundaries

Pilot only Maya, serially, in a non-production tenant/namespace with synthetic data. Hermes-on-Orgo is Phase A's sole owner. Eve Phase B uses a separate test project, Slack app/channel, inbox fixture, schedules disabled by default, secrets, receipt namespace, and no production Command Center write credential. If eve receives a bounded lease to Maya's Orgo computer, Hermes must first be quiesced and fenced; otherwise use a sanitized browser fixture (`../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Runtime Ownership” and “Eve Role”).

The pilot may read synthetic mail, create a synthetic work item, classify evidence, prepare a Slack reply/email draft, park for synthetic approval, and write test receipts. It may not send external mail, post to production Slack, publish, pay, schedule crews, submit permits, mutate trust tiers, change permissions, use service-role credentials, access unrelated mail, access Rowan/internal data across the boundary, install ungated skills/MCPs, provision/clone desktops, migrate databases, or activate production schedules.

Use one pre-approved test team/channel, test sender allowlist, and test persona. Do not rely on display names. Run process-kill, token-expiry, provider-failure, prompt-injection, approval-replay, duplicate-event, wrong-tenant, and compromised-browser drills. Promotion beyond synthetic scope requires a new human decision backed by the completed gate and zero P0/P1 failures; rollout remains serial, never fleet-wide.

## Kill Switch and Recovery

Provide independently operable kill switches for global Roofing-Ops, persona, trigger class (Slack/mail/cron/browser), outbound channel, provider, and credential. The switch must stop new effects server-side even if the worker is compromised; disabling a UI schedule alone is insufficient.

On suspected compromise:

1. Deny outbound effects and new work at the policy gateway; revoke the affected lease and increment its fencing epoch.
2. Disable Slack subscriptions/schedules/mail watches/browser automation for that principal; quarantine queued work. Do not start a replacement or alternate runtime.
3. Preserve immutable receipts, effect ledger, lease state, security logs, config hashes, and a forensically safe snapshot. Avoid collecting raw secrets or unnecessary message bodies.
4. Inventory the exact tenant, computer, browser profile, OAuth grants, bot/app, mailbox, provider keys, Command Center identity, and external effects. Reconcile reserved effects against vendor receipts.
5. Revoke sessions and rotate only implicated credentials first, expanding scope when evidence requires it. Remove persistence, unapproved extensions/skills/hooks, and stale workers. Invalidate approvals and browser cookies associated with the epoch.
6. Restore pinned, scanned configuration into the existing reconciled computer or a clean approved replacement only after inventory. Verify OS ownership, workspace scope, egress, and absence of cross-persona state.
7. Run read-only health checks, negative scope tests, injection tests, and one synthetic canary. A healthy process without current lease, cursor, receipt sink, or scope proof remains unhealthy.
8. A named human incident commander explicitly reacquires ownership with a new epoch and resumes one quarantined item at a time. Record the decision and monitor for duplicate or delayed effects.

For eve rollback, disable its test Slack connection and schedules, drain/checkpoint, reconcile effects, revoke its lease and credentials, then restore Hermes ownership and canary. For Hermes compromise, stop its gateway before repair; do not make eve an emergency co-owner. Slack identity failure must fail posting closed; Google expiry parks for human reauthentication; receipt-sink failure, scope mismatch, duplicate detection, or spend breaker stops all effects. This follows `../../round1/maya-orgo-hermes-runtime/maya-runtime.md`, “Failure and Recovery,” with the added requirement that containment be enforced outside the worker.

## Recommendation

Accept Round 1 as an **architecture hypothesis, not security authorization**. Block production and any live inbox/Slack/browser connection. First complete one combined A3 and evidence packet for the Maya Hermes-on-Orgo synthetic baseline, close P0-1 through P0-3 and P1-1 through P1-5, and obtain explicit human approval. Then run the bounded adversarial pilot above. Only after Hermes establishes a safe measured baseline should eve receive its separate isolated comparison.

Keep Cursor and Devin out of the Roofing-Ops persona plane. Consider them later only through separate tool-gate reviews for low-risk DevTeam or browser-adjunct work. The production decision should remain **Hermes baseline retained** unless eve matches every identity, scope, approval, egress, receipt, tenancy, and recovery control and materially reduces operator recovery toil. Any cross-tenant access, wrong-principal post, approval bypass, duplicate external effect, financial error, secret leak, or failed fencing event is an automatic rejection.
